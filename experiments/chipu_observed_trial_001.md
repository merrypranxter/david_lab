## Hypothesis
Operation order can change downstream forms while a common invariant survives. This is a deterministic operator/conformance demonstration, **not** evidence of a new generative mechanism.

## Fixed rules
- A: replace every `p` with `f`.
- B: replace `f` with `v` only between vowels `a/e/i/o/u`.
- A+B: A then B.
- CHILD: B then A; **manually recombined**, not emitted by DAVID LAB's breeding engine.
- ABLATION: CHILD minus B, therefore A alone.
- Invariant: `mora` must not change.
- Seeds fixed **before execution**: 1847, 2026, 31415, 42069.
- Each seed selects five words from seven source forms, plus `mora`, without replacement.

## Descriptive observations (not fitness labels)

- 20 observed outputs (4 matched sets of five conditions). No planned output was marked as observed.
- A+B and CHILD differ at 14 of 24 corresponding word positions.
- Removing B from CHILD changes 3 of 24 positions. For seed 42069 it changes **zero** because all sampled non-anchor source words lack a pre-existing intervocalic `f` for B to modify before A.
- `mora` survives in all 20 outputs.
- ABLATION equals A in every seed, as the specified rules require.

## What the recorder made visible
An ablation is only informative when its target rule has an opportunity to act. The 0/6 ablation result for seed 42069 is not proof that B is unnecessary in general: that input contains no eligible initial `f`. A follow-up study should deliberately stratify words by whether B can act before A, while keeping the provenance and matched task stable.

## What this cannot establish
No AI was asked to improvise. These are deterministic string transformations, not generated artworks, human-blinded assessments, or machine-discovered novelty. The repeated seed conditions use overlapping samples from a small lexicon, so they do not provide independent evidence about model robustness or originality.
